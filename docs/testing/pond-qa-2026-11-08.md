# Monets Lily Pond: exploration, UX and multiplayer QA

Tested November 8, 2026, against the local web app, PostgreSQL and presence worker.
The design reference was `.agents/PROJECT.md` §1: belonging, gentle discovery and
unhurried small efforts. The bank remains a place to explore through its objects
and ripples, with keyboard names available to assistive technology.

## Changes made from the walkthrough

- Simplified fishing to one main action. Guests see free worms; members open a
  compact bait pouch when needed. Optional wagering lives inside that pouch.
  Removed locked guest bait buttons, the inactive sign-in badge and the floating
  guest sign-in prompt. Instructions and error feedback share the control bar.
- Kept routine catches out of the ambient announcement queue and bounded its
  queue, remembered IDs and timer bookkeeping.
- Fixed Space on settings accidentally starting a cast, Enter on a bank object
  opening chat, focus escaping the activity, and Tab dismissing a catch reveal.
  Escape closes nested settings before leaving fishing. Returning to the bank
  restores focus to the fishing ripple.
- Fixed holding through the hook: continuing to hold now reels immediately.
  Easy Reel persists across sessions on the device.
- Kept the angler's WebGL rig alive across orientation and layout changes and
  withheld the rod until its attachment was ready, avoiding a detached rod during
  loading or resizing. The initial portrait layout uses the actual viewport.
- Stopped drawing the covered bank and avatars while fishing, while continuing
  networking and room ownership updates.
- Wait for server ownership before opening an online fishing spot. A reproduced
  simultaneous-claim race previously let both visitors enter the same spot.
- Completed the presence worker's WebSocket close handshake. Previously a closed
  connection could remain CLOSING in the browser and delay reconnecting.
- Handle a spot being occupied during disconnection: cancel the stale fishing
  attempt, explain the conflict inside the existing control bar, and change its
  action to Back to bank.

## Browser and interaction coverage

Playwright Chromium, including real browser keyboard events and CDP touch input:

| Viewport | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| 1440 × 900 | Bank exploration, keyboard controls, complete guest catch/reveal |
| 390 × 844 | Bank exploration, touch casting/hooking/reeling, member and guest catches |
| 320 × 568 | Small-phone layout and complete guest touch catch |
| 844 × 390 | Landscape layout and rotation |
| 768 × 1024 | Tablet resize and rod attachment |

Walked to and opened the noticeboard, bait shop and journal. Verified Escape,
keyboard object activation without opening chat, and touch dragging without
horizontal document scroll or accidentally entering fishing. Fishing controls
remain inside the viewport. Checked normal and reduced motion.

Ten fishing entry/exit cycles on desktop and ten on phone passed with no page
errors and a stable 13 textures after every cycle. Focus stayed inside fishing
and returned to the ripple afterward. Rotation retained the same angler canvas
and a correctly attached visible rod.

Touch-to-charge feedback sampled approximately 28 ms at normal CPU speed and
7 ms at 3× CPU throttling; both fishing samples reported 60 FPS. These are two
observations, not a latency percentile benchmark or evidence that throttling is
faster. A missed bite showed inline feedback and returned to idle at both rates.

Guest fishing passed through charging, flight, waiting, bite, holding to hook,
reeling, landing, reveal and repeat on desktop, phone and small phone. A member
also completed a real server-backed catch and its journal reflected the catch.
Tab did not dismiss the reveal. Bait selection, pouch collapse, unaffordable
purchase feedback, settings and Easy Reel persistence were checked.

Aborting a member cast request showed “The pond could not be reached.” in the
control bar, returned to idle, and allowed a successful retry. Invalid bait,
reel ticket, bait purchase, exchange and bounty requests returned 400, with no
server errors.

## Multiplayer and load

Tests used isolated pond instances and independent browser contexts, away from
the normal visitor instance. Checked movement/depth updates, occupied ripples,
spot release, reconnect/reclaim, reconnect after another visitor took the spot,
and simultaneous claims with injected claim delay. The connected race regression
ended with exactly one visitor fishing. The reconnect conflict left the new
owner fishing and gave the returning visitor Back to bank.

Eight visitors shared one instance: two browser clients and six authenticated
WebSocket clients sending movement/depth updates four times per second. A ninth
browser received the full response and joined the next instance. Both original
browsers observed all six synthetic departures. No browser page errors occurred.

Authenticated pond state, journal, bounties, feed and leaderboard reads:

| Concurrency | Requests | Successful | p50 | p95 | Maximum |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 60 | 60 | 15 ms | 47 ms | 3941 ms |
| 8 | 60 | 60 | 114 ms | 148 ms | 166 ms |
| 24 | 60 | 60 | 253 ms | 339 ms | 399 ms |

The sequential maximum includes cold development route compilation. This is a
local responsiveness check, not a production throughput claim.

## Practical limits

These checks used headless Chromium, local networking and software graphics.
Single-visitor bank samples were typically about 46–55 FPS; the eight-visitor
case with two browsers rendering concurrently measured about **23–25 FPS**.
That crowded-bank result is a remaining performance risk, not a 60 FPS pass.
The covered-bank optimization reduces work during fishing; it does not establish
that every crowded bank is smooth on every device.

Mobile viewport emulation is not physical iOS/Safari or Android hardware testing.
Production latency, mobile GPU/thermal behavior, audible sound quality and
subjective human discoverability still need hands-on checks. The walkthrough
does not claim production-wide capacity or universal device performance.
